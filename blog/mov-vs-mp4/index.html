MOV vs MP4: what's the difference, and which should you use?

iPhones record MOV, the rest of the world prefers MP4. Learn how the two containers differ, why conversion is often lossless, and when it matters.

Your iPhone saves videos as .mov files. Upload forms, Windows apps and many websites ask for .mp4. It's natural to assume these are completely different formats — but they're far more alike than they look, and that's good news for quality.

Containers vs codecs

A video file has two layers. The codec (such as H.264, HEVC or ProRes) is how the pictures and sound are compressed. The container (MOV or MP4) is the box that holds the compressed streams together with timing information, metadata, chapters and subtitles.

MOV is Apple's QuickTime container from 1991. MP4 is the international MPEG-4 Part 14 standard — and it was literally based on the QuickTime file format. The structure of the two is nearly identical.

In everyday iPhone videos, both containers hold the same HEVC or H.264 video and AAC audio. The differences show up at the edges: MOV handles Apple's professional formats such as ProRes and some HDR metadata better, while MP4 is the format every platform has agreed to accept.

Is converting MOV to MP4 lossless?

Often, yes. If the video inside the MOV uses a codec MP4 supports (H.264 or HEVC), the streams can be copied into an MP4 container without re-encoding — a process called remuxing. It's nearly instant and the picture is bit-for-bit identical.

Re-encoding is required when:

  • The MOV contains ProRes or another codec MP4 players won't handle.
  • You also want to compress, change resolution, or switch HEVC to H.264 for compatibility.
  • You're trimming at points that don't line up with keyframes and need frame accuracy.

When to use each

Keep MOV when…

  • You're editing in Final Cut Pro, iMovie or LumaFusion.
  • The video is ProRes, Log or Dolby Vision HDR and you want to preserve that.
  • It's staying in your Apple Photos library.

Use MP4 when…

  • Uploading to a website, form or learning platform.
  • Sharing with Windows or Android users.
  • Playing on smart TVs, game consoles or in-car systems.
  • Embedding in a web page or presentation.

What about M4V and 3GP?

M4V is Apple's near-twin of MP4 used by the TV app. Unprotected M4V files convert to MP4 instantly. 3GP is the old mobile-phone format from the 2000s; converting 3GP to MP4 rescues those memories for modern devices.

A word on HEVC compatibility

Even inside an MP4, HEVC video may not play on older Windows PCs without Microsoft's extension, or in some web browsers. If someone tells you your MP4 "has no picture" or "won't play", re-export with H.264 in LocalConvert's options. The file will be a little larger, but it will play virtually anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Does renaming .mov to .mp4 work?

Sometimes, because the containers are so similar — but it's unreliable. Some players check the internal structure and refuse the file, and metadata like rotation or HDR may be misread. A proper remux takes the same few seconds and produces a valid MP4.

Is MP4 lower quality than MOV?

No. Quality is decided by the codec and bitrate, not the container. A remuxed MP4 contains exactly the same video data as the MOV it came from.

Which is smaller?

When they hold the same streams, MOV and MP4 files are within a fraction of a percent of each other. If you need a smaller file, compress it — changing the container alone won't help.

Do I lose subtitles or chapters?

Basic iPhone recordings have neither. Professionally produced MOV files can carry chapter markers and subtitle tracks; MP4 supports both, though some players only show subtitles in specific formats. If subtitles matter, keep a separate SRT file alongside the video — it works with almost every player.

What about Live Photos?

A Live Photo is a HEIC still plus a short MOV clip. Export the video part from Photos and convert it to MP4 or a GIF with Video to GIF.

Bottom line

MOV and MP4 are siblings. Keep MOV for your library and editing; convert to MP4 when the video is leaving the Apple ecosystem. With the right tool the conversion takes seconds, usually loses nothing, and never needs to touch the internet.
