How to make a PDF from photos on iPhone (4 ways)

Turn receipts, IDs, homework or scans into one tidy PDF on iPhone — built-in tricks plus a faster way with page order, page size and compression.

Forms ask for "a single PDF". Expense systems want receipts as PDFs. Teachers want homework as one document, not twelve photos. Your iPhone can do all of this — some methods are hidden, and some are much faster than others.

Method 1: Images to PDF in LocalConvert (most control)

This is the method we designed for exactly this job, so it handles the details that matter: page order, page size, margins and file size.

  1. Open LocalConvert and go to Tools › Images to PDF.
  2. Select your photos — from Photos or Files, in any format including HEIC and RAW.
  3. Drag the thumbnails to set the page order.
  4. Choose a page size: Fit to image, A4 or US Letter, and optional margins.
  5. Tap Create PDF, then save to Files or share.

Everything happens on your iPhone, which matters when the photos are of passports, bank statements or medical letters. See JPG to PDF and HEIC to PDF for format-specific notes.

Method 2: Scan with the Files or Notes app

If the paper is in front of you, scanning beats photographing. In the Files app, tap the ⋯ menu and choose Scan Documents; in Notes, tap the camera button and choose Scan Documents. iOS detects page edges, flattens perspective and saves a multi-page PDF.

Scanning is excellent for paper. It can't help with photos you've already taken, and you have limited control over file size.

Method 3: The print preview trick

A long-standing hidden feature: select photos in the Photos app, tap Share › Print, then pinch outward on the page preview. iOS turns the print job into a PDF you can share or save.

It works, but it's fiddly: page size follows your printer region, margins are fixed, and large batches create very large files.

Method 4: Save to Books

Sharing photos to the Books app creates a PDF in your library. It's simple, but the result lives inside Books, and getting it back out to email or a form takes extra steps.

Keep the PDF small enough to send

A PDF made from twelve full-resolution iPhone photos can exceed 40 MB — too big for many upload portals that cap files at 5 or 10 MB. Two fixes:

  • Choose the Balanced quality preset when creating the PDF; photos are resized to a sensible print resolution.
  • Run the finished file through Compress PDF, which re-encodes embedded images while keeping text sharp.

Uploading to a government or university portal? Check the size limit first and pick a compression level that lands comfortably under it.

Fixing mistakes after the fact

Forgot a page or put them in the wrong order? You don't need to start again:

  • Organize PDF lets you drag pages, rotate sideways scans and delete extras.
  • Merge PDFs combines your new PDF with another document.
  • Split PDF extracts just the pages someone asked for.

Tips for great-looking photo PDFs

  • Shoot documents in even daylight, avoiding your own shadow.
  • Crop away the table edges before converting — Crop & Rotate helps.
  • Use A4 or Letter for anything that might be printed; use Fit to image for screen-only documents.
  • Name the file clearly (for example, *2026-10 Receipts – Travel.pdf*) before sending.

With A4 or Letter, each photo is scaled to fit inside the page and centred, so nothing is cropped. Add a small margin (around 12–18 points) if the document will be printed; most printers can't print to the very edge.

Mixing formats and sources

Real-world document piles are messy: a HEIC from your camera roll, a PNG screenshot of a confirmation email, a JPG someone sent you, a RAW photo from a camera. LocalConvert accepts them all in one batch and normalises them into consistent PDF pages. You can also start from an existing PDF and add photos to it by creating a PDF from the photos and then using Merge PDFs.

Frequently asked questions

Is text in a photo PDF searchable?

A PDF made from photos contains images of text, not text itself, so you can't search or copy it like a typed document. Scans made with the Files or Notes scanner can include recognised text. For forms and receipts, an image-based PDF is usually exactly what's expected.

Can I password-protect the PDF?

On iPhone, Pages and some PDF editors can add a password. If you're sending sensitive documents, consider sharing through an end-to-end encrypted channel rather than relying on a PDF password.

Why is my PDF sideways?

Photos store orientation as metadata, and some tools ignore it. LocalConvert applies the orientation when building pages; if a page still looks wrong, rotate it in Organize PDF.

Summary

For paper, scan with Files or Notes. For photos you already have — especially many of them, in mixed formats, with privacy concerns — use Images to PDF in LocalConvert, then compress if the destination has a size limit.
