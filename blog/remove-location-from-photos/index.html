How to remove location data from photos before you share them

Every iPhone photo can reveal exactly where it was taken. Learn what's hidden in photo metadata and how to strip GPS location before posting or sending.

When you take a photo, your iPhone records far more than the image. Hidden inside the file is metadata — known as EXIF — that can include the precise GPS coordinates where you stood, the date and time to the second, and the exact device you used. Share that original file with a stranger and you may be sharing your home address.

Videos carry similar information, including location, in their metadata.

Do social networks remove it?

Many large social platforms strip location metadata from the public copy of images they display. But you can't rely on that everywhere: messaging apps that send "original quality" files, email attachments, cloud-storage share links, forums, marketplaces and AirDrop to strangers may all deliver the untouched file. The safest habit is to remove location yourself before sharing anything sensitive.

Option 1: Turn off location for one share (built into iOS)

  1. Select the photos in the Photos app and tap Share.
  2. At the top of the share sheet, tap Options.
  3. Turn off Location (and optionally All Photos Data), then tap Done.
  4. Share as normal.

This works for that share only, and only through the share sheet. It's easy to forget.

Option 2: Remove location from the photo in your library

In Photos, open a picture, swipe up (or tap ⓘ), tap the location, and choose No Location. This edits the library copy. It's useful for individual photos but slow for many.

Option 3: Make clean copies with LocalConvert

Selling something online? Photograph the item away from your front door and strip location before uploading. Backgrounds and metadata are both clues.

Option 4: Stop recording location in the first place

Go to Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services › Camera and choose Never. New photos won't contain GPS coordinates at all. The trade-off: you lose the Places map in Photos and location-based Memories, which many people enjoy. A middle path is to keep location on for your private library and remove it when sharing.

What about screenshots?

Screenshots don't contain GPS location, but they include the date and device. More importantly, check what's visible: notification banners, battery level and carrier name, map previews and other people's names in chats can reveal more than metadata ever could.

Don't hand the problem to a website

Plenty of websites offer to "remove EXIF data online". The irony is that you must upload the original — including its location — to a server you know nothing about to have the location removed. Doing it on-device avoids that entirely. More on why that matters.

How to check what a photo contains

You can't fix what you can't see. On iPhone, open a photo in Photos and swipe up: you'll see the date, the device and lens, and a map if location was recorded. For a fuller picture — including altitude, direction and software — open the file in LocalConvert's File Inspector, which lists every metadata field it finds in plain language.

It's worth checking photos you've received, too. A picture someone sends you as a file may reveal where *they* were, which is a good reminder of what your own photos can reveal.

Videos need attention too

Video files from iPhone include a location tag in their metadata just like photos. The same share-sheet Options toggle applies when sharing from Photos, and LocalConvert's Remove Location & Metadata tool handles MOV and MP4 files as well as images. Remember that the content of the video — street signs, house numbers, school uniforms — can reveal location even when the metadata is clean.

Frequently asked questions

Does removing metadata reduce image quality?

No. LocalConvert rewrites the metadata without re-compressing the picture when the format allows, so the pixels stay exactly the same.

Will Photos still sort my clean copies by date?

If you remove all metadata, the capture date goes too, and Photos will sort the copy by the date it was added. To keep the date but drop only the location, use the share sheet's Options › Location toggle described above, or edit the location to No Location in Photos.

Do AirDrop and Messages send location?

When you share from Photos, iOS includes location by default unless you turn it off in Options. Recipients can then see where the photo was taken.

Checklist before you share

  • Is the location in the metadata? Remove it.
  • Is anything identifying in the background? Crop it.
  • Does the recipient need full resolution? If not, compress or resize.
  • Are you sending the original file or a link? Links can expose more.

Thirty seconds of care keeps your address, routines and devices private — and with on-device tools, nobody else ever sees the original.
