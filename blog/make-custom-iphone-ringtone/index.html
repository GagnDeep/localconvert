How to make a custom iPhone ringtone from any song or video

Create an iPhone ringtone from a song, voice memo or video: trim the perfect section, export M4R, and set it as your ringtone — step by step.

The default ringtones are fine — until you're in a room where five phones play Opening at once. A custom ringtone makes your phone instantly recognisable, and it can be anything: a chorus, a podcast catchphrase, your child's laugh from a video. Here's how to make one properly.

What an iPhone ringtone needs to be

  • Format: M4R — AAC audio in an MPEG-4 container with a ringtone extension.
  • Length: 40 seconds or less for ringtones (alert and text tones are best kept under 30 seconds).
  • Rights: use audio you own or have permission to use. DRM-protected Apple Music tracks can't be converted.

Step 1: Get the audio

Your source can be almost anything:

  • A song file you own (MP3, M4A, WAV, FLAC) saved in Files.
  • A Voice Memo — share it to Files first.
  • A video — LocalConvert can extract the audio from any clip in Photos.

Step 2: Create the M4R with LocalConvert

  1. Open LocalConvert and go to Tools › Ringtone Maker.
  2. Choose your song or video.
  3. Drag the handles to select up to 40 seconds. Tap play to preview the loop.
  4. Add a short fade-in and fade-out so it doesn't start abruptly.
  5. Tap Make ringtone and save the M4R to Files.

You can also convert directly, for example MP3 to M4R or M4A to M4R, and use Trim Audio for precise edits.

Pick a section with a distinctive start — the first two seconds are what you'll hear most. Choruses and instrumental hooks work better than verses.

Step 3: Install the ringtone

On iPhone directly

Recent versions of iOS let you share a short audio file from the Files app and choose Use as Ringtone. If you see that option in the share sheet, that's all you need.

Using GarageBand (works on any iPhone)

  1. Install GarageBand (free) and open it.
  2. Create a new project with any instrument, tap the Tracks view, then the Loop button and browse Files to import your audio.
  3. Drag it onto a track, then go back to My Songs.
  4. Long-press the project, tap Share › Ringtone, name it and export.

Using a Mac

Connect your iPhone, select it in the Finder sidebar, and drag the M4R file onto the iPhone's General tab. It appears in Settings › Sounds & Haptics › Ringtone.

Step 4: Set it

Go to Settings › Sounds & Haptics › Ringtone and pick your new tone at the top of the list. To give one person their own ringtone, open their contact, tap Edit › Ringtone, and choose it — the easiest way to know who's calling without looking.

Choosing the perfect clip

A ringtone is heard in a very specific way: in short bursts, often from a pocket, over background noise. What sounds great on headphones can be mush through a phone speaker. A few principles help:

  • Start strong. Many calls are answered within a few seconds, so the opening is what you'll actually hear. Begin on a beat, a vocal hook or a distinctive riff.
  • Favour the mid-range. Deep bass disappears on small speakers. Guitars, vocals, pianos and synth leads cut through.
  • Make it loop gracefully. If a call rings for longer than your clip, it repeats. Ending on a phrase that leads back into the start sounds intentional.
  • Mind the volume. Quiet intros and long fades make you miss calls. Keep the fade-in short — half a second is plenty.
  • Think about the room. Your ringtone will go off in meetings, cinemas and quiet trains. Choose something you won't be embarrassed by.

Ringtones from videos and voice recordings

Some of the best ringtones aren't songs at all. A clip of your dog barking, a friend's catchphrase, or your kid saying "Daddy, phone!" makes calls from a specific person instantly recognisable. In LocalConvert, pick the video in Photos and the Ringtone Maker extracts the audio automatically. For voice recordings, the waveform view makes it easy to cut out the silence before and after.

If your source is a long recording — a podcast episode or a concert video — use Trim Audio or Trim Video first to get roughly the right section, then fine-tune in the Ringtone Maker.

What about Android or other phones?

Android phones use ordinary MP3 or OGG files as ringtones. If you're sharing a ringtone with a friend on Android, convert your clip to a standard format such as M4A or WAV instead of M4R.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the limit 40 seconds?

iOS only lists ringtones up to 40 seconds long. Longer files are silently ignored, which is the most common reason a new ringtone "doesn't show up".

Can I use an MP3 directly?

Not as a ringtone. iPhone ringtones must be AAC audio with the .m4r extension. Converting MP3 to M4R takes a second and keeps the sound virtually identical.

Make a text tone too

Short sounds — a two-second clip — make great text and notification tones. Use the same steps and keep it brief so it doesn't annoy you twenty times a day.

With your audio trimmed and converted on-device, the whole process takes about a minute, and your recordings never leave your phone.
